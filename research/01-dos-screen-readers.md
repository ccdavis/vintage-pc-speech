# DOS Screen Readers: Survey for a FreeDOS Port on a 386 DX-25 + Sound Blaster 32

Research date: 2026-09-19. Target: an open-source screen reader for FreeDOS on a real 386 DX-25 with an SB32 (SB16-compatible DSP), fed by a software synthesizer we write ourselves.

**How to read this.** "Verified" means I fetched the page or downloaded and inspected the archive during this session. "Recalled" or "unverified" means it comes from search-result snippets, second-hand summaries, or memory. All downloaded archives were unpacked into the session scratchpad and inspected; nothing was installed.

---

## 1. Executive summary

- **Exactly one DOS screen reader has published source under a free license: PROVOX 7.03** (Kansys / Charles E. "Chuck" Hallenbeck), GPL v2-or-later, written in Eric Isaacson's **A86 assembler dialect**, ~150 KB of source in 10 files. Verified by downloading and reading the source and docs. It is what SvarDOS ships today in its "talking" edition.
- **ASAP** (MicroTalk / Larry Skutchan) is the best-regarded DOS screen reader, pure assembly, ~30 K resident, but **no source exists publicly** and its license text is proprietary. Binaries circulate freely (HAPP archive "demo" of Jan 2002; Vetusware says demo limits are lifted; the FreeDOS community obtained informal permission to redistribute it on a live CD). No Sound Blaster support.
- **JAWS for DOS 2.31** is officially freeware (Henter-Joyce disclaimer file verified), no source. **Tinytalk 1.67** is shareware (unregistered copy fully functional, redistributable), no source, written in Turbo C++ and TASM. **Vocal-Eyes 3.0**: only a 20-minute-limited demo was found; the "made free" claim is **not verified**. **Flipper 5.0** circulates as abandonware with a Sound Blaster module.
- **Sound Blaster software speech in the DOS era meant First Byte SmoothTalker/Monologue (SBTALKER.EXE + BLASTER.DRV)**; Tinytalk, JAWS and Flipper all had SBTALKER back-ends. It used ~180 K RAM and Henter-Joyce called it unusable ("it will not silence when asked"). Its API is undocumented but has been reverse-engineered (systoolz/dosbtalk, Apache-2.0).
- **The universal integration point is the hardware-synth protocol.** Every screen reader can drive a synth over BIOS COM/LPT ports; several auto-detect a DoubleTalk PC at I/O 29Eh/29Fh; and the Braille 'n Speak serial protocol is what the modern FreeDOS/SvarDOS accessibility work standardised on (emubns). The DoubleTalk PC/LT command set and register interface are fully documented (RC Systems developer's tools, verified).
- **Recommendation:** port **Provox** (source + GPL), and make the software synthesizer speak the **DoubleTalk ^A command set** behind an INT 14h "simulated COM port" (exactly how RC Systems' own DTPRN.COM worked), which also makes it usable unmodified by ASAP, JAWS, Tinytalk and Vocal-Eyes. Details in section 6.

## 3. Product-by-product findings

### 3.1 PROVOX 7.03 (Kansys, Inc. / Charles E. Hallenbeck) — **source available, GPL**

- **Vendor/author:** "PROVOX 7.xx - Created 1985 by Kansys, Inc. / Copyright (c) 1999 Chuck Hallenbeck" (sign-on string in `9PROVOX7.A`). Author: Charles E. Hallenbeck, Ph.D., Hudson NY (READ.ME). Kansys was at 1016 Ohio, Lawrence KS; an early listing gives Provox at $295 supporting DECtalk, Votrax PSS, SynPhonix 200 and Echo (Columbia Kermit `aablind.hlp`, from a search snippet — unverified). Wikipedia lists the vendor as "Kansys, Inc." with license "uncertain"; that is wrong, see next point. The user's recollection of "Bob Zapata" did not surface anywhere; the author is Hallenbeck.
- **License (verified):** `provox7.doc` section "Terms And Conditions": "This program is free software; you can redistribute it and/or modify it under the terms of the GNU General Public License ... either version 2 of the License, or (at your option) any later version."
- **Language/build (verified, READ.ME):** PROVOX7.EXE is A86 assembly (Eric Isaacson's shareware A86 v3.22), linked with Borland TLINK 7 (`a86 +os *.a` then `tlink -k provox7`). The control utility PV7.EXE is one C++ file (`PV7.CPP`, Borland C++ 4.51) that links against **RC Systems' proprietary `DTC.LIB`** (DoubleTalk C library) — that library is in the zip but is not GPL. The zip also bundles A86.COM and its manual (A00–A17.DOC, "Copyright 1986–1990 Eric Isaacson", shareware). Files: `0PREFACE.A 1I5CODE.A 2I8CODE.A 3I9CODE.A 4I9JUMPS.A 5OTHERS.A 6I16CODE.A 7I22CODE.A 8I47CODE.A 9PROVOX7.A`, dated Jul–Dec 1999, ~150 KB total. The author explicitly asks for "freeware or shareware tools to replace the commercial linker and compiler".
- **Download:** <https://allinaccess.com/happ/files/provox7.zip> (265,803 bytes). Also a ready QEMU image: <http://emubns.sourceforge.net/svarog386-qemu-provox.zip>.
- **Architecture (verified from source):** Loads either as a **character device driver** (`DEVICE=PROVOX7.EXE` in CONFIG.SYS; has a real device header, strategy/interrupt entries) or as an ordinary TSR. Hooks **INT 05h** (PrtSc), **INT 08h** (timer: every other tick reads the BIOS data area — mode 40:49, columns 40:4A, page offset 40:4E, cursor 40:50, rows 40:84 — then scans **B800:0000 / B000:0000** directly for "smart screen" change detection, box detection, and attribute filtering), **INT 09h** (hardware keyboard: review commands via a prefix key, default `/`, numeric keypad in "enhanced mode"), **INT 10h** (video: captures BIOS teletype/char writes for "screen echo"), **INT 16h** (keyboard echo), **INT 17h** (saved for LPT output), **INT 2Fh** (multiplex ID F1h for PV7 control; also watches Windows 1605h/1606h). Separate 256-byte stacks per interrupt. Buffers: 60 lines × 82 bytes text buffer, 512-byte clipboard, 10 "screen banks" × 10 subscreens with per-app filters (WordPerfect, WordStar, ProComm, etc. preconfigured).
- **Synth interface (verified):** `PV7 <DEVICE> [port]`; devices: `ACCENT <LPT3>, AUDAPTER <COM1>, BNS <COM1>, BRLMATE <COM1>, DECTALK <simulated COM4>, DTLT <COM#>, DTPC (direct I/O), LITETALK <LPT1>, PORTTALK <LPT3>, PSS <COM1>`; ports LPT1–4, COM1–4. Output goes through **BIOS INT 14h** (init AX=00E3h = 9600 8N2; AH=1 write after checking CTS via AH=3 and XON/XOFF via AH=2) or the saved **INT 17h** vector for parallel, **direct port I/O for DoubleTalk PC** (`dt2output`: wait status bit 4 = RDY, `out`, wait), and the DEC driver via **INT 2Fh AH=D0h** for DECtalk PC (functions 3 status, 21 pause, 22 resume, 23 flush). Per-synth tables (`vox1code`…`vox10code`) only map punctuation/control characters to spoken names; per-synth init strings (`'0H/'` PortTalk, `'50/5M1/'` DTPC/LiteTalk, `'F11/'` Audapter, `'7/'` PSS, `'5/5/'` Accent, `'0/'` DECtalk PC) and silence strings (^X for PortTalk/DoubleTalk, `ESC q.` for Votrax PSS, `ESC =x` for Accent). As Eric Auer noted on freedos-user (verified, msg20758): Provox does no text-to-phoneme work; "PROVOX seems to expect that the speech synth indeed has local CPU power and firmware to convert English text to speech itself".
- **Memory:** PROVOX7.EXE is 24,141 bytes; resident image ≈ code + ~5 K text buffer + stacks — roughly 25–30 K (estimate, not measured). Doc says to load after EMM386/QEMM so MEMMAKER can load it high.
- **386 compatibility:** 8086-class code, no protected-mode tricks; runs in SvarDOS (Mateusz Viste, freedos-user 2024-11-19, verified msg26607) and in FreeDOS under QEMU (emubns image). Should be fine on a 386 DX-25.
- **Current use:** SvarDOS's accessible edition "uses the PROVOX screen reader and requires a Braille 'n Speak synthesizer connected to the COM1 port; if running as a VM, the BNS can be emulated" (search snippet from svardos.org; site fetch refused connection during this session, so treat the wording as unverified though consistent with the verified mailing-list post).

### 3.2 ASAP — Automatic Screen Access Program (MicroTalk / Larry Skutchan) — **no source**

- **Vendor/years:** MicroTalk, Louisville KY; manual "Copyright 1991, 2001", latest build 2002-01-10 (ASAP.COM 54,696 bytes). Skutchan left APH to found MicroTalk (APH blog, verified: <https://www.aph.org/blog/larry-skutchan-access-tech-visionary/>). Prices in 1992: ASAP Soft $525, Classic $795 with DoubleTalk PC, Portable $895 with LiteTalk (RDC newsletter Mar–Apr 1992, verified).
- **Language/memory (verified, manual):** "ASAP is written in assembly language, so its memory requirements are minimal"; RDC 1992: "about 30K", loads high with LH; NFB 1992 review: "approximately 40K".
- **Status:** License text in the manual is proprietary ("You may not distribute copies to others"). In practice: HAPP hosts the Jan-2002 build as a periodic-nag demo; Vetusware states "all demo limitations have been lifted" (verified page text, not verified by running it); the 2020 freedos-user thread and Joseph Norton's FreeDOS 1.3 RC3 live CD indicate MicroTalk gave permission for distribution on that CD (thread summary; the exact permission wording was not visible in what I fetched). Treat as "freely circulating, not formally freeware". **No source code found anywhere.**
- **Download:** <https://allinaccess.com/happ/files/asapdemo.exe> (a ZIP: ASAPINST.EXE, PORT.EXE, SYN.DAT, ASAP-NEW.DAT (inner zip with ASAP.COM, ASAP.TXT manual, `*.TBL`, `*.SET`), ASAP.HTM manual).
- **Synthesizers (verified from SYN.DAT and manual Appendix A):** Accent SA/PC/Messenger; APH PocketBraille; Apollo/Juno; Audapter; Braille 'n Speak/Braille Blazer/Type 'n Speak (9600 8N1, software handshake, "speech box mode"); DECtalk external, DECtalk internal-serial, DECtalk PC (via DEC's DT_DRIV driver), DECtalk Express; DoubleTalk PC (**auto-detected**), DoubleTalk LT, LiteTalk, TripleTalk PCI (TTALKDOS.COM); Echo PC/GP external, internal Echo/Micro Echo (via Textalker `TALK LPT3` port emulation); KeyNote Gold; Kurzweil Personal Reader, MultiVoice; Reading Edge; Sounding Board (SBLOAD.COM); SynPhonix 210/215 & VoTalker (SONIXTTS/SONIX+TTS); PortTalk devices (TransPort, BrailleMate); "Other" = generic. **No Sound Blaster / SmoothTalker entry** (STHING.TBL for the Speech Thing exists but is an empty generic template).
- **Documented driver interface (verified):** the manual's "Using ASAP with Other Synthesizers" plus `GENERIC.TBL`/`USER.TBL`: a plain-text table, one code per line between slashes — init string, kill-speech, temporary-silence, pitch 1–9, speed 1–9, volume 1–9, user functions 1–9 — used as `ASAP generic com1|lpt1`. Example: `LT.TBL` (DoubleTalk family) = init `7b 2x`, pitch `10p`…`90p`, speed `1s`…`9s`, volume `1v`…`9v`, extras `1o`…`4o`, `55f`…`9f`; `DECTALK.TBL` = `[:dv ap N]`, `[:ra N]`, `[:dv gv N]`, `[:nX]` voice names; `BNS.TBL` = init `Z`, pitch `1P`…`40P`, speed `7E`…`15E`, volume `1V`…`9V`. So any synth that looks like a COM/LPT device and accepts ASCII text plus a small command set can be plugged in without touching ASAP.

## 4. How DOS screen readers work (verified against the Provox source; consistent with Tinytalk/ASAP docs)

1. **Residency.** A TSR or a CONFIG.SYS character device driver (Provox, Tinytalk 1.63+, DECtalk/Accent drivers). Loading as a device driver lets it speak CONFIG.SYS-time messages. Provox reserves private stacks for each hooked interrupt and uses an INT 2Fh multiplex ID (F1h) so a separate control program (PV7.EXE) can change settings.
2. **Screen access.** Text-mode video RAM at **B800:0000** (colour) or **B000:0000** (mono, mode 7) is read directly: 2 bytes per cell (char, attribute), 80×25 default; Provox honours the BIOS data area for mode (40:49h), columns (40:4Ah), page offset (40:4Eh), per-page cursor (40:50h) and rows (40:84h) so 43/50-line modes work. A **timer hook (INT 08h)** polls the screen every other tick to detect changes ("smart screen"), boxes, light bars and attribute-filtered regions. An **INT 10h hook** catches BIOS teletype/character writes (AH=09h/0Ah/0Eh) so scrolling console output is spoken as it happens ("screen echo").
3. **Cursor tracking.** Provox uses the BIOS cursor position (40:50h). The alternative technique — reading CRTC cursor registers 0Eh/0Fh via 3D4h/3D5h (3B4h/3B5h mono) — catches applications that move the hardware cursor without BIOS; I did not find it in Provox (general knowledge, not verified in any source here). Screen readers also implement a **software cursor**: tracking a highlighted attribute (ASAP "Use Software Cursor", Tinytalk light-bar tracking, Vocal-Eyes light-bar windows).
4. **Keyboard.** **INT 09h** (hardware) for review commands and a "prefix" or hot-key scheme, plus **INT 16h** (BIOS keyboard services) for typing echo; the review cursor ("independent cursor", "reading cursor", "VE cursor") moves over the screen buffer without disturbing the application; "route cursor" stuffs keystrokes to move the app cursor (Provox "paste" stuffs the BIOS keyboard buffer).
5. **Speaking.** Text is sent verbatim to a "smart" synth that does its own text-to-phoneme conversion; the screen reader only expands punctuation names, handles repeats ("3 dashes"), caps/number modes, and inserts control sequences for rate/pitch/volume/silence. Output paths: **BIOS INT 14h** serial (Provox: AH=00 init 9600 8N2, AH=03 status → check CTS, AH=02 read → honour XON/XOFF, AH=01 write), **BIOS INT 17h** parallel, **direct I/O** for card synths (DoubleTalk PC at 29Eh/29Fh), or a vendor **TSR driver API** (DECtalk PC's DT_DRIV over INT 2Fh AH=D0h; Artic PortTalk/Sonix; Textalker `TALK LPT3` port emulation; GW Micro's Sounding Board driver; Vocal-Eyes' own .COM drivers). "Dumb" boards (SSI-263 based Accent Mini, SynPhonix, Sounding Board, Votalker, Echo PCII) need a memory-resident text-to-phoneme driver from the vendor; "smart" ones (DoubleTalk, DECtalk, Accent PC/SA, Audapter, BNS) do it on board (Tinytalk SYNINFO.DOC).
6. **Indexing** (optional): the reader embeds markers and the synth reports them back as it speaks, so continuous reading can stop at the right word; DoubleTalk and DECtalk support it; JAWS/Tinytalk exploit it.

## 5. Synthesizer protocols we could target

- **DoubleTalk PC / LT / LiteTalk / TripleTalk (RC Systems) — fully documented (verified, RC Systems "DoubleTalk Developer's Tools" 1992–96, <http://www.cs.uni.edu/~mccormic/4740/documentation/DoubleTalk_Tools.pdf>).** Commands are `^A` (01h) + decimal argument + letter: `nP` pitch 0–99, `nS` speed 0–9, `nV` volume, `nF` formant, `nX` tone, `nE` expression, `nB` punctuation level, `nA` articulation, `nR` reverb, `nO` voice, `nI` index marker 0–99 (echoed back as a binary byte), `nG` protocol options, `@` reinitialise, `?` interrogate (returns serial number, ROM version, all settings, terminated by 7Fh); `^X` (18h) stops speech and flushes; text is spoken when a CR or NUL arrives (except Character mode); modes Character/Phoneme/Text. **PC card:** two adjacent 8-bit I/O ports, base (LPC/index port, idle value 7Fh) and base+1 (TTS port; factory 29Eh/29Fh). TTS status byte: bit 6 SYNC (speaking), bit 5 SYNC2 (early), bit 4 RDY (may write), bit 3 AF (<300 bytes free), bit 2 AE (<300 bytes queued). Driver writes a byte only when RDY=1, except `^X`, which should be written unconditionally. **LT:** 9600 8N1, DTR handshake, sync on the RI modem-status line (RI=0 while talking) or software sync (ASCII `B`/`E`). RC Systems' own **DTPRN.COM** (<1 K) hooks INT 14h/17h to make DoubleTalk look like any COM1–4/LPT1–3, storing the real I/O address in the BIOS port table at 40:00h–40:0Ch; there is also `INT4DAPI.COM`, an "IBM Speech Adapter BIOS emulator" with an assembly API (Function 05h TTS: AL=1 Speak, DS:SI buffer, CX length; AL=2 silence). ASAP autodetects a DoubleTalk PC; Provox, Tinytalk (TTDOUBLE), JAWS and Vocal-Eyes (DBLTALK.COM) all drive it. There is also a firmware-accurate emulator (daiverd/doubletalk-pc) to compare behaviour against.
- **Braille 'n Speak "speech box mode"** (Blazie): 9600 8N1, software handshake; ASAP's table shows `nP` pitch, `nE` speed, `nV` volume and init `Z` (verified BNS.TBL); the exact stop/flush and other control codes were not visible in any page I fetched — the definitive reference is the emubns C source (svn://svn.code.sf.net/p/emubns/code/, not fetched). This is the protocol SvarDOS + Provox use today, so a BNS-compatible synth would be immediately useful to that community.
- **DECtalk** (DTC-01/PC/Express): `[:ra N]` rate 75–650, `[:dv ap N]` pitch, `[:dv gv N]`/`[:vol set N]` volume, `[:nX]` voices (P,H,F,D,B,R,U,W,K,V), `[:punct none]`, `[:er ig]`, `[:index mark N]`, `[:sync]`, `[:phoneme arpabet on]`; Ctrl-C flushes (verified in-line command reference <https://rmdir.de/~michael/DECtalk_Express/rongemma/shrt_cmt.htm> and ASAP's DECTALK.TBL). DECtalk PC used a DOS TSR presenting a pseudo COM/LPT port and an INT 2Fh AH=D0h API (Provox source).
- **Accent** (Aicom, SSI-263): silence `ESC =x` (Provox); other codes not collected. **Votrax PSS:** silence `ESC q.`; `!`/`@` are command characters (Vocal-Eyes doc). **Sounding Board** (GW Micro): `Ctrl-E R0–9` rate, `P`, `V`, `T`; `Ctrl-X` silence (Vocal-Eyes GENERIC.DAT example). **PortTalk** (Artic): `^X` silence; init `0H`. **Textalker** (Street Electronics) `TALK LPT3` emulates a printer port.
- **Sound Blaster software speech:** SBTALKER.EXE + BLASTER.DRV (First Byte SmoothTalker v3, SB 1.x/2.0/Pro era; Dr. Sbaitso and SAY.EXE sit on it; loads into EMS if available; ~180 K conventional otherwise). Monologue (v4) had swappable drivers for SB, Covox, Tandy, IBM Speech Adapter. Creative **TextAssist** (SB16-ASP/AWE32, Windows 3.1) needed the ASP chip and was not used by DOS screen readers (search-derived; unverified). The SBTALKER API is undocumented; dosbtalk reimplements it in C for Turbo C 2.01. Every screen reader that "supported the Sound Blaster" (Tinytalk TTBLAST, JAWS J_SBLAST, Flipper f_blast) simply talked to SBTALKER — there is no direct-DSP speech path in any screen reader found.
- **Public-domain PC-speaker TTS:** SPEECH.COM/SAY.COM (Douglas Sisco / Andy McGuire, 1987+), INT F1 with DS:BX → `$`-terminated string; INT F2 variant. Quality is poor but the API is a model of minimalism.

## 6. Ranking for a port

**1. PROVOX 7.03 — port this.**
- *License/source:* the only candidate with source, and it is GPL-2.0-or-later. The one wrinkle is `PV7.CPP` linking RC Systems' `DTC.LIB`; replace PV7 with a small tool that talks to the resident driver through its documented INT 2Fh (AH=F1h) interface, dropping DoubleTalk-library calls.
- *Architecture:* ~150 KB of A86 assembly in 10 well-commented files; timer-poll + INT 10h/09h/16h hooks; table-driven per-synth punctuation and init/silence strings; no text-to-phoneme code (that lives in the synth, i.e. our job). Build needs A86 (shareware, bundled) and TLINK; translating to NASM/JWasm is mechanical but real work (A86 idioms: `if e mov ...`, `> l1` local labels, decimal `int 20`, `w`/`b` size prefixes).
- *Synth interface:* adding an eleventh "vox" entry (init string, silence routine, punctuation table, output via INT 14h or a far-call/INT 2Fh API) is a few hundred lines. Or add nothing and let our synth be a *simulated COM port* — Provox already recommends "a simulated COM port" for internal boards and its DECTALK entry is defined as "simulated COM4".
- *Risk:* 1999 code; Auer called the dialect "rather exotic"; no maintainer; but it runs today in SvarDOS/FreeDOS under QEMU, and the FreeDOS/SvarDOS community (Viste, Norton, Lewellen) is actively interested.

**2. ASAP (binary-only) — use as the compatibility/reference target, not the port base.**
- Best UX and smallest, pure assembly, "works flawlessly in virtual machines"; its `GENERIC.TBL` and auto-detection of DoubleTalk PC mean a synth that emulates a COM port or the DoubleTalk PC command set works with zero changes. But: no source, proprietary license text, and the "freeware" status rests on informal permission and Vetusware claims. Useful to validate our synth against, and as a fallback if the Provox port stalls.

**3. Tinytalk 1.67 (binary-only) — third.** Smallest footprint (27 K), explicitly redistributable unregistered, C+asm design that is easy to reason about from Bohlman's paper, and `TTDOUBLE.EXE` / `TTSOUND.EXE` give two protocol targets (DoubleTalk, PortTalk). No source. JAWS for DOS is a legitimate freeware alternative but is the largest and most complex, and Vocal-Eyes' only free form is a 20-minute demo.

**Synth-protocol recommendation.** Implement the software synthesizer as a TSR that (a) accepts plain ASCII plus the **DoubleTalk `^A` command set** and `^X` flush, and (b) presents itself as a **BIOS serial port via an INT 14h hook** (DTPRN-style: AH=0 init, AH=1 write, AH=2 read returning "no data", AH=3 status with CTS/DSR set so XON/XOFF-aware readers keep sending). That one interface is consumed unmodified by Provox (`PV7 DTLT COM4` or `PV7 DECTALK`), ASAP (`ASAP lt com4`), Tinytalk (TTDOUBLE), JAWS (DoubleTalk external) and Vocal-Eyes (DBLTALK.COM /LT). A BNS mode (init `Z`, `nP/nE/nV`) is cheap to add for SvarDOS compatibility. Emulating the DoubleTalk PC's I/O ports is *not* feasible from a real-mode TSR (no port trapping without V86), so prefer the serial-emulation route on the 386. Budget: SmoothTalker's 180 K was the reason screen-reader vendors rejected SB speech; on a 386 DX-25 with an SB16 DSP the synthesis (formant or diphone/LPC, 8-bit 8–11 kHz via SB16 auto-init DMA) must run in well under a tick's worth of CPU per phoneme and keep conventional-memory use small (UMB/EMS for tables), or the result will be as unusable as SBTALKER was.
